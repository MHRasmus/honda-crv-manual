Front Brake Pad Inspection and Replacement
Frequent inhalation of brake pad dust, regardless of material composition, could be hazardous to your health.
  • Avoid breathing dust particles.
  • Never use an air hose or brush to clean brake assemblies. Use an appropriate vacuum cleaner.

Inspection - NISSIN Type
  1. Raise the front of the vehicle, and support it with safety stands in the proper locations.
  1. Remove the front wheels.
  1. Check the thickness (A) of the inner pad (B) and outer pad (C). Do not include the thickness of the backing plate.
    Brake pad thickness:
    Standard:
    11.5−12.2 mm (0.45−0.48 in.)
    Service limit:
    1.6 mm (0.06 in.)

  1. If the brake pad thickness is less than the service limit, replace all of the front brake pads as a set.
  1. Clean the mating surface of the brake disc and the inside of the wheel, then install the front wheels.
Replacement - NISSIN Type
  1. Remove some brake fluid from the master cylinder.
  1. Raise the front of the vehicle, and support it with safety stands in the proper locations.
  1. Remove the front wheels.
  1. Remove the flange bolt (A) while holding the caliper pin (B) with a wrench. Be careful not to damage the pin boot, and pivot the caliper (C) up out of the way. Check the hose and pin boots for damage and deterioration.
  1. Remove the brake pads (A) and the pad shims (B).
  1. Remove the pad retainers (A).
  1. Clean the caliper bracket (B) thoroughly; remove any rust, and check for grooves and cracks.
  1. Check the brake disc for damage and cracks.
  1. Apply Molycote M-77 grease to the retainers on their mating surfaces (indicated by the arrows) against the caliper bracket.
  1. Install the pad retainers. Wipe excess assembly paste off the retainers. Keep any assembly paste off the discs and pads.
  1. Mount the commercially available brake caliper piston compressor (A) on the caliper body (B).
  1. Press in the piston with the brake caliper piston compressor so the caliper will fit over the brake pads. Make sure the piston boot is in position to prevent damaging it when pivoting the caliper down.
    NOTE: Be careful when pressing in the piston; brake fluid might overflow from the master cylinder's reservoir. If brake fluid gets on any painted surface, wash it off immediately with water.
  1. Remove the brake caliper piston compressor.
  1. Apply Molycote M-77 grease to the pad side of the shims (A), the back of the brake pads (B) and the other areas indicated by the arrows. Wipe excess assembly paste off the pad shims and brake pads. Contaminated brake discs or brake pads reduce stopping ability. Keep grease and assembly paste off the brake discs and brake pads.
  1. Install the brake pads and pad shims correctly. Install the brake pad with the wear indicator (C) on the upper inside. If you are reusing the brake pads, always reinstall the brake pads in their original positions to prevent a temporary loss of braking efficiency.
  1. Pivot the caliper down into position. Install the flange bolt (A), and tighten it to the specified torque while holding the caliper pin (B) with a wrench. Be careful not to damage the pin boot.
  1. Clean the mating surface of the brake disc and the inside of the wheel, then install the front wheels.
  1. Press the brake pedal several times to make sure the brakes work.
    NOTE: Engagement may require a greater pedal stroke immediately after the brake pads have been replaced as a set. Several applications of the brake pedal will restore the normal pedal stroke.
  1. Add brake fluid as needed.
  1. After installation, check for leaks at hose and line joints or connections, and retighten if necessary.
    Test-drive the vehicle, then check for leaks.
Inspection - BOSCH Type
  1. Raise the front of the vehicle, and support it with safety stands in the proper locations.
  1. Remove the front wheels.
  1. Check the thickness (A) of the inner pad (B) and outer pad (C). Do not include the thickness of the backing plate.
    Brake pad thickness:
    Standard:
    12.4−13.2 mm (0.49−0.52 in.)
    Service limit:
    2.0 mm (0.08 in.)

  1. If the brake pad thickness is less than the service limit, replace the front brake pads as a set.
  1. Clean the mating surface of the brake disc and the inside of the wheel, then install the front wheels.
Replacement - BOSCH Type
  1. Remove some brake fluid from the master cylinder.
  1. Raise the front of the vehicle, and support it with safety stands in the proper locations.
  1. Remove the front wheels.
  1. Remove the flange bolt (A). Be careful not to damage the pin boot. Pull the caliper (B) outside and pushing back the piston to make the clearance (C) between the brake disc and the outer pad.
  1. Raise the caliper (A) with an appropriate flat-tip screwdriver (B), and pivot the caliper up out of the way.
  1. Remove the brake pads (A).
  1. Remove the pad retainers (A).
  1. Clean the caliper bracket (B) thoroughly; remove any rust, and check for grooves and cracks.
  1. Check the brake disc for damage and cracks.
  1. Install new pad retainers. Wipe excess grease off the retainers. Keep grease off the brake discs and the brake pads.
    NOTE: Do not apply excess grease to the new retainers.
  1. Clean and degrease the outer supporting part (A).
  1. Mount the commercially available brake caliper piston compressor (A) on the caliper body (B).
  1. Press in the piston with the brake caliper piston compressor so the caliper will fit over the brake pads. Make sure the piston boot is in position to prevent damaging it when pivoting the caliper down.
    NOTE: Be careful when pressing in the piston; brake fluid might overflow from the master cylinder's reservoir. If brake fluid gets on any painted surface, wash it off immediately with water.
  1. Remove the brake caliper piston compressor.
  1. Clean the caliper thoroughly; remove any rust, and check for grooves and cracks.
  1. Peel off pattern paper from the sticky shim (A), and stick and press it to the shim surface of the outer pad.
    The sticky shim is made of stretch material and is not detachable. Make sure to stick it carefully.
    NOTE:
    • Make sure that no grease content on the shim surface.
    • Align the form of the sticky shim to the shim as shown.
    • If adhering any oil, grease, dust, metal debris, and other foreing material to the sticky shim surface, replace the sticky shim as new one.
  1. Wipe excess grease off the pads. Contaminated brake disc or pads reduce stopping ability. Keep grease off the brake disc and the brake pads.
  1. Install the brake pads (A) correctly. If you are reusing the pads, always reinstall the brake pads in their original positions to prevent a momentary loss of braking efficiency.
    NOTE:
    • Do not hold the sticky shim when installing the brake pad.
    • Keep any contaminant off the sticky shim.
    • Return the piston fully so as to prevent damaging the sticky shim.
    • Before install the brake pads, make sure that no grease content was left on outer supporting part (B).
  1. Peel the backing paper of the sticky shim (C) that was attached to the outer pad.
  1. Pull the caliper body (A) to make the gap on outer pad side.
  1. Pivot the caliper down into position. Install the flange bolt (B), and tighten it to the specified torque.
    NOTE: Be careful not to damage the sticky shim.
  1. Press the brake pedal several times to make sure the brakes work.
    NOTE: Engagement may require a greater pedal stroke immediately after the brake pads have been replaced as a set. Several applications of the brake pedal will restore the normal pedal stroke.
  1. Add brake fluid as needed.
  1. Clean the mating surface of the brake disc and the inside of the wheel, then install the front wheels.
  1. Start the engine, and press the brake pedal 10 seconds with 196 N (20 kgf, 44 lbf) of force to apply the sticky shim securely.
  1. After installation, check for leaks at hose and line joints or connections, and retighten if necessary.
    Test-drive the vehicle, then check for leaks.
